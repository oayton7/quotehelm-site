# LinkedIn posts: playbook, topic bank and post log

How Pillion Web uses LinkedIn to reach trades business owners in West Sussex, East Sussex and Hampshire without paying for ads. Agents draft with the `linkedin-post` skill (`.claude/skills/linkedin-post/SKILL.md`); other assistants, such as Grok, should follow the same rules. Oscar posts everything himself. Prepared 18 November 2026.

## The short version

- Write for one trades owner, about one moment on the job, in plain first-person English.
- Hook in the first two lines, 120 to 220 words, short lines, a question at the end.
- Name real places from the three counties in the text, and use three to five hashtags.
- Put the link in the first comment, pointing to the most relevant guide or trade page.
- Post from Oscar's personal profile where possible and reshare to the company page, because a page with few followers reaches very few people.
- Reply to every comment in the first hour.
- No invented clients, results or statistics. Price wording: "fixed prices from £225" only. No technology names.

These are widely reported practices, not guarantees. The post log below is how we find out what works for Pillion Web.

## What the research says (checked 18 November 2026)

Most of these sources are marketing blogs and some repeat each other, so read the numbers as direction, not fact.

**LinkedIn reach**

- Organic reach has fallen. One widely cited analysis (Richard van der Blom) reports views down about 50% and engagement down about 25% year on year. Dwell time (people stopping to read) and real comments matter more than likes.
- Format ranking in 2026 benchmarks: document carousels about 6.6% average engagement, native video 5.6%, image posts 3.2%, text only 2.0%. Polls get seen but get very little engagement. One analysis says images do best for accounts under 5,000 followers.
- External links in the post body are reported to cut reach sharply (figures quoted range from about 50% to 70%). Some sources now say the link-in-first-comment workaround no longer helps much either. One outlier source disagrees.
- Personal profiles get much more organic reach than company pages; one source puts company page organic reach at about 1 to 2%.
- The first 60 to 90 minutes matter, and comments that start a back-and-forth count most. Yes or no questions rarely start a conversation.

Sources: [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm), [Perroly](https://www.perroly.com/blog/linkedin-organic-2026-what-works), [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-how-it-works), [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/best-performing-content-on-linkedin), [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now), [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/how-to-create-meaningful-engagement-on-linkedin-3-tips/).

**Market notes for post angles**

- Small builders reported fewer enquiries in the first half of 2026: enquiries, workloads and employment all turned negative, with enquiries falling most ([FMB State of Trade](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/state-of-trade-survey-h2-2025.html), [Build News](https://www.buildnews.co.uk/london-builders-report-flat-workloads-as-new-enquiries-fall/)). Angle: when enquiries are scarcer, losing one to a missed call costs more.
- Word of mouth is still how most homeowners find trades, but they commonly check the firm online (Google, reviews) before ringing. Angle: a referral still looks you up first.
- About half of homeowners say they do not trust tradespeople (FMB and TrustMark, 2025). Angle: a clear quote form, real reviews and matching Google details build trust before the first call.
- An older Fix Radio survey (2022) found about a third of tradespeople had lost work because they were too busy on site to answer. Widely repeated figures such as "£24,000 a year lost to missed calls" come from vendor blogs without published methods: do not use them.
- Not verified here: what is trending in Oscar's own feed. Agents cannot see LinkedIn feeds. Oscar can screenshot local posts that did well, and an agent will analyse them.

## Audience question bank

Open questions trades owners can answer with a short story. Use one per post, at the end. Rotate trades.

1. What's the worst time your phone has rung on a job? Up a ladder, under a sink, halfway through a pour?
2. When a quote request comes in, what's the one detail you always have to chase?
3. Missed calls or messy quote requests: which costs you more work?
4. How do most of your new customers find you now: word of mouth, Google, Facebook, or a directory?
5. Do you call back missed numbers the same day, or do the evening session? What's worked?
6. What's one question every customer should answer before you go out to price a job?
7. Has a customer ever told you they picked you because of your Google reviews?
8. Roofers: after a storm, how do you decide who to call back first?
9. Scaffolders: what's the detail homeowners never know to tell you (pavement licence, access, neighbours)?
10. If your website disappeared tomorrow, would you notice in your enquiries?
11. What do you wish homeowners understood before asking for a quote?
12. What's the busiest month for enquiries round here, and are you ready for it?

## Voice example: the first post

Posted on the Pillion Web company page by Oscar, about 10 to 11 November 2026, with an image of scaffolders on a roof. Strong points: a specific hook, a scene trades owners recognise, one simple fix, a closing two-way-choice question.

To improve next time:

- Post from the personal profile. The page had one follower, so the post could only reach a handful of people whatever its quality.
- The third paragraph (about starting Pillion Web) sits between the hook and the problem and slows the read. Move the introduction to the end or a later post.
- Seven hashtags, two of them general (#SmallBusinessUK, #Scaffolders duplicating #Scaffolding). Use three to five, and include #EastSussex or an East Sussex town.
- A missing full stop after "barely works on a phone".
- The image looked like a stock or generated picture. Oscar's own photo, or a simple carousel, is likely to hold attention longer.
- "Sharing all things Pillion Web" signals company news. Promise something useful to the reader instead, for example a weekly tip for trades firms.

```text
Most scaffolders lose work before they've even quoted.

Not because they're bad at the job. Because they're three storeys up and the phone is in the van.

I've just started Pillion Web, alongside my day job. I help trades businesses in Chichester, West Sussex and Hampshire win more work from the website they already have.

I'm starting with scaffolders, because the problem is clear. Someone needs scaffold for a roof job. They ring a few firms. Nobody picks up, because everyone's on site.

Two things quietly cost scaffolders work:

Calls missed while the crew is up the scaffold or on the road.

Quote requests stuck in an inbox, or sent through an email link that barely works on a phone

Neither needs a new website.

A simple quote form that asks for the address, the type of job and a photo of the access gives you what you need to price it. No phone tag.

This is my first post, and I'll be sharing all things Pillion Web going forward.

Scaffolders, which one costs you more work: the missed calls or the messy quote requests?

#Scaffolding #Scaffolders #TradesBusiness #SmallBusinessUK #Portsmouth #Westsussex #Hampshire
```
