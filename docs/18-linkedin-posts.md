# LinkedIn posts: playbook, topic bank and post log

How Pillion Web uses LinkedIn to reach trades business owners in West Sussex, East Sussex and Hampshire without paying for ads. Agents draft with the `linkedin-post` skill (`.claude/skills/linkedin-post/SKILL.md`); other assistants, such as Grok, should follow the same rules. Oscar posts everything himself. Prepared 18 November 2026.

## The short version

- Write for one trades owner, about one moment on the job, in plain first-person English.
- Hook in the first two lines, 120 to 220 words, short lines, a question at the end.
- Name real places from the three counties in the text, and use three to five hashtags.
- Put the link in the first comment, pointing to the most relevant guide or trade page.
- Post from Oscar's personal profile where possible and reshare to the company page, because a page with few followers reaches very few people.
- Reply to every comment in the first hour.
- No invented clients, results or statistics. Price wording: "fixed prices from £225" only. No technology names.

These are widely reported practices, not guarantees. The post log below is how we find out what works for Pillion Web.

## Voice example: the first post

Posted on the Pillion Web company page by Oscar, about 10 to 11 November 2026, with an image of scaffolders on a roof. Strong points: a specific hook, a scene trades owners recognise, one simple fix, a closing question. To improve next time: post from the personal profile (the page had one follower), name an East Sussex place, and use three to five hashtags instead of seven.

```text
Most scaffolders lose work before they've even quoted.

Not because they're bad at the job. Because they're three storeys up and the phone is in the van.

I've just started Pillion Web, alongside my day job. I help trades businesses in Chichester, West Sussex and Hampshire win more work from the website they already have.

I'm starting with scaffolders, because the problem is clear. Someone needs scaffold for a roof job. They ring a few firms. Nobody picks up, because everyone's on site.

Two things quietly cost scaffolders work:

Calls missed while the crew is up the scaffold or on the road.

Quote requests stuck in an inbox, or sent through an email link that barely works on a phone

Neither needs a new website.

A simple quote form that asks for the address, the type of job and a photo of the access gives you what you need to price it. No phone tag.

This is my first post, and I'll be sharing all things Pillion Web going forward.

Scaffolders, which one costs you more work: the missed calls or the messy quote requests?

#Scaffolding #Scaffolders #TradesBusiness #SmallBusinessUK #Portsmouth #Westsussex #Hampshire
```
