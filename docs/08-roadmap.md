# Ninety day roadmap

The dates below are relative to starting work. They are planning targets, not promised sales or delivery outcomes. Keep one active build at first and adjust to actual availability.

| Period | Work | Exit evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–7 | Choose one offer, begin interviews, settle working assumptions, create a test environment | Two-sentence pitch, draft scope, customer conversations and initial working flow |
| Days 8–14 | Complete test system, recovery tests, cost record and handover draft | Passing required tests and a real demonstration video |
| Days 15–30 | Complete ten conversations, qualify buyers and seek one or two paid pilots | Paid agreement or explicit learning about why owners decline |
| Days 31–60 | Deliver and measure; aim for three paid installations; refine documentation | Delivery effort, usage and outcomes recorded consistently |
| Days 61–90 | Standardise repeatable work, refine price, build one acquisition channel and publish permissioned evidence | A repeatable sales/delivery process with understood margin and support demand |

## Weekly review

Keep a short private operating note: conversations, qualified problems, proposals, cash collected, hours spent, failed notifications, support effort and next decision. Link public documentation changes to the relevant learning without publishing client details.

## Decision gates

- **No one pays:** inspect need, trust, timing and offer before building more features.
- **Customers pay but delivery overruns:** narrow supported platforms/integrations, improve onboarding or change price.
- **Delivery works but customers do not act:** improve the handover/owner workflow or change the segment.
- **Support consumes the contribution:** revise scope, monitoring and the recurring model.
- **Several customers use the same workflow successfully:** standardise templates and procedures, then test the next acquisition channel.

Three customers are an initial learning gate, not market-fit proof. Only consider a standalone platform after repeated unmet needs justify its development and support cost.
