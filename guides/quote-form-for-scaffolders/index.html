What does a quote form do for a scaffolder?

A quote form asks customers for the details you need to price a scaffold, then sends them to your phone by text and email straight away. Here is what it asks, what happens next, and what it will not do.

By Oscar Ayton, Pillion Web. Published 10 November 2026.

What does a quote form do for a scaffolder?

It gives customers a short form on your website that asks for the details you need to price a scaffold, then sends them to your phone by text and email straight away. Every enquiry is also saved, so none get lost.

Without one, enquiries tend to arrive as a missed call with no message, or an email that says “how much for scaffolding?” and nothing else. Both need a call back before you know whether the job is worth pricing.

What does the form ask?

Whatever you need to decide whether to price the job. You choose the questions and approve the wording.

  • Name, phone number and the address or postcode.
  • What the scaffold is for, such as a roof, chimney, painting or an extension.
  • How many storeys and which sides of the building.
  • Whether it will go on the pavement or road, which may need a council licence.
  • When it is needed and roughly how long for.
  • Whether the enquiry is from a homeowner, builder, roofer or other contractor.

Keep it short. Every extra question makes some people give up, so ask only what changes your answer.

What happens after a customer sends it?

You get a text and an email with their answers, the customer sees a message saying it has been received, and the enquiry is saved in a list you can look back through.

That means you can read the job on your phone between sites and decide whether to ring back now, later or not at all.

How is it different from a contact form or an email address?

A contact form usually sends one email, often to an inbox you check at night. A quote form asks the questions you need and gets the answers to your phone straight away.

If your current contact form works, test it: fill it in on your phone and see whether, and where, it arrives. See the missed enquiries checklist for other quick checks.

What will a quote form not do?

It will not price the job for you, and it does not guarantee more work.

It makes it easier for customers to reach you with useful details, and makes sure you see the enquiry. Winning the job is still down to your reply and your price.
