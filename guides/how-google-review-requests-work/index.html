How do Google review requests work?

After a job, you send the customer a short message with a direct link to leave you a Google review. Here is when to ask, what to say, and the rules to stay inside.

By Oscar Ayton, Pillion Web. Published 10 November 2026.

How do Google review requests work?

After a job, you send the customer a short text or email with a direct link to leave you a Google review. They tap the link, choose a star rating and write a few words.

Google gives every business with a Business Profile a share link for reviews. You find it in your profile under the option to ask for reviews. Putting that link in the message saves the customer from having to search for you.

When is the best time to ask?

Soon after the job is finished and the customer is happy with it, while it is fresh in their mind.

A short message the same day or the next day works better than a request weeks later. One polite reminder is fine. More than that starts to feel like pressure.

What should the message say?

Thank them, say a review helps a small local firm, and include the link. Keep it to two or three lines.

For example: “Thanks for choosing us for your roof. If you have a minute, a Google review really helps a small local firm. Here is the link.” Use your own words.

What are you not allowed to do?

Do not ask only the customers you expect to be happy, do not offer anything in return, and never write reviews yourself or ask friends to.

  • Ask everyone. Google’s rules do not allow selectively asking for positive reviews, or discouraging negative ones.
  • No rewards. Google does not allow discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews.
  • No fake reviews. Since April 2025, UK consumer law has banned fake reviews and hiding the fact that a review was paid for or rewarded. Google can also remove reviews or restrict a profile that breaks its rules.

Should I reply to reviews?

Yes, briefly and politely, including the bad ones.

Other customers read the replies. A calm reply to a poor review that explains what you did to put it right often says more about you than the review itself.
