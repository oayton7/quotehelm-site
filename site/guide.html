Oscar start here

Tandem’s first job is to sell and deliver one useful improvement to a real business: a website enquiry reaches the owner, is recorded and can be acted on. Use a paid pilot to establish demand, delivery capability and cost before expanding the catalogue.

The website concept is a sales starting point. The interactive example is fictional and does not connect to an automation service. The documentation supplies a plan; it does not mean the operating business is already configured.

What is decided

  • Working parent brand: Tandem, broad enough for more services and products later.
  • First service to test: enquiry capture and phone/email notifications on an existing trades website.
  • Initial market hypothesis: small plumbing and heating firms around Chichester and West Sussex, initially focused on scheduled work.
  • Build on existing tools where suitable. Do not start by writing a custom CRM.
  • Keep the original Quotehelm site available as reference while refining the new direction.

What you need to decide

  1. Whether interviews support that customer group and problem.
  2. Which platforms you can reliably install on and what access is required.
  3. The exact pilot scope, price, ongoing charges, support and ownership terms.
  4. Whether Tandem is usable after domain, business-name and trademark checks.
  5. Which hosting and form arrangements will support the eventual live site.

See the decision register. A working name is not evidence of trademark availability.

Tandem business plan

Executive decision

Launch a focused implementation service, establish repeatable delivery and customer demand, then productise the parts that repeat. The first product is a defined service with a repeatable setup and support process. A standalone software platform is a later possibility, not a launch requirement.

Customer and problem

The initial hypothesis is a small plumbing/heating business in West Sussex with an existing website, regular enquiries, and an owner who spends much of the day working away from a desk. Start with scheduled installations, bathrooms and servicing. Emergency response adds commitments that the first offer does not need.

The problem to validate is fragmented enquiry handling: messages arrive in different places, useful details are missing, and owners forget what needs attention. A practical installation can collect the right information, notify the right person and leave a record.

This does not create demand by itself. Enquiry volume depends on relevant visitors and enquiry conversion; jobs won also depend on suitability, owner response, pricing and capacity. A business with no traffic, no capacity or no willingness to respond may be a poor first customer.

Value proposition

“I set up and test a simple enquiry system on the website you already have, so requests reach your phone and are ready for a callback.”

Customers pay for diagnosis, installation, integration, testing, handover and agreed support. A form or AI model alone offers little differentiation. The proposition must be demonstrated through real delivery evidence.

Market and competitors

The initial market is deliberately local and narrow so Oscar can learn quickly and build references. No defensible market-size estimate or proven local demand is established by this review. Validate with ten owner conversations and paid pilots before wider geographic targeting.

Local agencies compete on websites and trust; inexpensive website providers set low price anchors; trades software offers many features already; automation services charge to run a defined workflow. Doing nothing is also a competitor. See the dated comparison.

The opportunity to test is reliable implementation around the customer’s existing process. If many owners already own suitable software, configure it instead of creating duplicate systems.

Revenue model

  • Fixed setup fee for an explicitly scoped installation.
  • Optional ongoing support with a clear time allowance and response window.
  • Necessary vendor subscriptions and usage either billed directly to the customer or explicitly included in a capped managed plan.
  • Later additional services only where the underlying data and triggers exist.

The proposed first model is customer-owned accounts and directly paid vendor charges, plus a setup fee and optional care. This is a proposed change from the current Quotehelm README, which says care covers tool costs. Resolve that difference before selling.

Pricing and unit economics

The current £345 form price can be a limited pilot hypothesis for a straightforward setup. After measuring total effort, test a higher standard price if necessary. A suggested £495–£695 range is an experiment, not established willingness to pay.

Count discovery, sales, access chasing, build, test, revisions, handover and support time. A service can appear profitable if founder labour is ignored while consuming all available capacity.

Cash and capacity

Agree a deposit and acceptance payment schedule before work begins; the exact split is a commercial decision, not fixed by this pack. Keep invoices, payment records and an allowance for tax. Do not subsidise unlimited usage or pay indefinitely for a customer who has cancelled care.

An example 15-hour week allocates five hours to customers/sales, six to implementation, two to tests/documentation and two to administration/content. Adjust based on actual availability. Begin with one active installation until delivery time is known.

Retainers are not passive income. Model support hours, supplier costs, churn, payment failures and account handovers. Major employment or spending decisions should follow sustained collected income and a personal financial assessment kept outside this repository.

Go to market

Start with introductions, owner conversations and narrow evidence-based audits. Use the website to explain and demonstrate the offer. Search content supports longer-term discovery; it is not the first-month sales forecast. Avoid automated outreach volume until the offer and contact rules are understood.

Product roadmap

  1. Enquiry capture, durable records and notifications.
  2. Quote follow-up when quote status and stop conditions are reliable.
  3. Review requests when job completion has a trustworthy trigger.
  4. Booking where calendars, capacity and confirmation are reliable.
  5. Bounded AI assistance after approved information and evaluation exist.

Do not build a full custom CRM initially. Consider custom software only after several customers demonstrate the same unmet need and existing tools cannot serve it economically.

Measure conversations, qualified problems, proposals, paid pilots, total delivery hours, contribution, notification reliability, owner response and continued use. Report counts and definitions, not vanity percentages from very small samples.

After three paid installations, decide whether to continue the same offer, narrow it, reprice it or change the customer segment. Three is a learning gate, not proof of market fit. Keep the reasoning in the decision register.

First offer and pricing

Proposed pilot

Enquiries to your phone: one quote-request form on one existing business website, with a durable enquiry record and notifications to one agreed phone number and one inbox. Include approved wording, a controlled test, basic handover and a defined initial defect-fixing period.

The £345 figure is the current Quotehelm setup price, retained as a pilot hypothesis. The prototype website does not yet implement the delivery stack. Do not advertise an unconditional three-day turnaround until platform compatibility, access, testing and capacity are established.

Use a written compatibility check before quoting. A form embedded on one platform may require a different paid plan or permissions on another. A completed quote-request form is not an instant quotation and must be described accurately.

Decide the recurring-cost model

Recommended starting model: the client owns essential accounts, pays necessary software and usage charges directly, and can choose a separate support service. Oscar has appropriately scoped access. Document how credentials, automations and data are handed over.

Alternative: a managed subscription that includes explicit usage limits, monitoring, support time and overage charges. In this model, the ongoing operating fee must be clear. It cannot be described as optional if the service stops without it.

For either option, answer: What remains operational if care is declined? What stops when care is cancelled? Who owns the phone number? Who pays the vendor? What export and migration help is included?

Care-plan specification

The legacy prices are £59/month for one or two products, £79 for three or more or a website, and £99 with chat or CRM. These are historical offer facts, not a validated Tandem cost model.

Before offering care, set:

  • Included checks and their frequency.
  • Monthly change/support allowance and whether unused time rolls over.
  • Response hours and acknowledgement target, distinct from resolution time.
  • Supplier outage handling and client notification.
  • Usage limits, exclusions and additional-work pricing.
  • Cancellation notice, account transfer and data export/deletion procedure.

Do not promise round-the-clock support around an availability schedule that cannot sustain it.

Later services

Quote follow-up requires an authoritative quote status and suppression on reply, acceptance, rejection or opt-out. Review requests need a completion event and a consistent eligible-customer policy. Booking requires live capacity and genuine confirmation. Missed-call replies require telephony integration and number/forwarding decisions. AI chat requires approved knowledge, escalation and evaluation.

Each additional service gets its own scope, test suite, cost model and support procedure before sale.

Correct pricing presentation

The original estimator permits a £1,095 website and its already-included £345 form to be selected together, producing £1,440. Reviews may also overlap depending on whether the package includes display alone or automated requests. Define bundles explicitly and stop duplicate charging.

The new concept uses one starting offer rather than the original estimator. The original pricing page is preserved and its defect still needs a separate fix before being reused. Keep prices, FAQs, structured data and any AI-facing summary consistent when approved terms change.

Build the test system and prove delivery

Working test case

Use a fictional scheduled bathroom-installation enquiry. A person submits contact details, a postcode and job description. The system stores the request, notifies a controlled phone and inbox, sends an agreed acknowledgement if included, and allows a responsible person to update its status.

The existing HTML demos simulate these actions. They are useful for explanation, but they are not evidence of storage, delivery, authentication, scheduling or failure recovery.

Candidate architecture

The original plan names Tally, Make and Twilio. Treat them as candidates to evaluate, not accounts that have already been configured:

  1. A form validates minimum contact, job and area information.
  2. An intake event receives a unique ID and is stored durably.
  3. An automation uses that ID to avoid duplicate notifications.
  4. Email and SMS notifications go to agreed destinations.
  5. Delivery state and failures are recorded separately from enquiry status.
  6. The owner updates the enquiry: new, contacted, quoted, won or closed.
  7. Failed jobs alert Oscar and can be replayed without duplicate customer messages.

Where the client’s existing CRM already supports the workflow, prefer its native capabilities. Confirm actual plan limits, integrations, delivery receipts, UK messaging costs, processor terms and account ownership before selecting suppliers.

Minimum record

Store enquiry ID, submission time, source, contact details, job description, postcode, acknowledgement state, notification state and owner status. Do not collect unrelated sensitive data. Define access roles and retention; do not commit actual submissions to GitHub.

Distinguish “accepted by a provider”, “delivered where supported”, “read” and “responded to”. Do not show a success message that promises inbox delivery merely because the form request succeeded.

Acceptance test suite

Use synthetic details and only phone numbers/inboxes controlled by the tester. Expected results must be written before running tests. Record pass/fail, timestamps, screenshots/log references and fixes. All required cases must pass before a client pilot, with any remaining limitations explicitly agreed.

Delivery procedure

  1. Discover the workflow and qualify the problem.
  2. Verify platform, access, supplier limits and ownership.
  3. Agree scope, data handling, costs, support and acceptance.
  4. Build in a separate test environment using synthetic records.
  5. Run the test suite and record failure recovery.
  6. Show the client the exact wording and behaviour; record approval.
  7. Deploy in an agreed window with a rollback plan.
  8. Run a controlled live test to agreed destinations.
  9. Hand over access, documentation and support contact details.
  10. Review use and operating cost after an agreed pilot period.

Evidence from the first pilot

Record a comparable baseline where feasible: number of valid enquiries, routes, human response times and how many received quotes. After installation, record the same definitions plus delivery failures and support effort. Keep sample sizes and dates visible.

Use separate measures for system notification latency and human response. Acknowledgement is not a sale. Revenue attributed to the system needs a defensible comparison; a before/after improvement alone may reflect seasonality, advertising or changed workload.

A useful early case study may say “all 18 recorded enquiries generated the agreed notifications during this period” if that is true. It cannot claim 18 additional jobs or enquiries on that basis.

Handover artefacts

  • A short video of the real test submission and notifications.
  • A diagram/list of providers and which account owns each step.
  • A setup and rollback procedure.
  • A failure-triage procedure with escalation contacts.
  • Actual vendor costs, usage assumptions and account limits.
  • Test results and approved customer-facing messages.
  • Export, access-transfer and offboarding instructions.

Store real client evidence in a controlled private location. Publish only permissioned, suitably minimised case studies.

Customer discovery and first sales

Qualify the problem

Begin with ten conversations, not a large automated campaign. Seek examples of real recent enquiries, delays and follow-up gaps. Owners who already have enough work, receive little website traffic, or will not respond to notifications may not benefit from the first offer.

Ask:

  1. Where did your last enquiry arrive, and what happened next?
  2. When did someone first respond? How do you know?
  3. Which messages or quotes still need action today?
  4. What software do you already pay for? Can you show the current process?
  5. What happens while you are busy or away?
  6. Which administrative job would you pay someone to improve?
  7. Who would use the system and who approves spending?

Use the discovery template. Ask about past behaviour before presenting a solution. Interest in AI is not evidence of a budget or an urgent problem.

First acquisition channels

Start with relevant introductions and referrals. Ask contacts to make an introduction where the business wants it. Offer a short walkthrough to an interested owner. Local partners such as web designers and business advisers may introduce a customer whose workflow needs help, but agree responsibilities and any referral terms openly.

For cold outreach, verify the business and subscriber type, record the source and reason for relevance, and keep a suppression list. Corporate-subscriber rules differ from sole-trader/individual rules; a company’s name alone does not establish who subscribes to an email or telephone service. Unknown status should be handled conservatively. UK GDPR may still apply to personal business-contact information. ICO B2B marketing guidance.

Sole traders and some partnerships generally require consent or a qualifying soft opt-in for marketing emails/texts. Do not treat a publicly listed address as consent. Social direct messages are not an automatic workaround. Screen telephone outreach under the applicable TPS/CTPS rules. Include identity and a working opt-out, and respect objections.

A bounded audit

Spend a small, agreed amount of time identifying one visible problem. Check mobile usability, the enquiry route, contact options and how much effort a visitor must make. Keep a screenshot and URL/date to support the observation.

A public audit cannot tell you how many leads a business loses, whether its inbox is monitored or its true conversion rate. Do not invent those conclusions. Avoid building a bespoke site for every non-responder; prepare a tailored example after interest is established.

Example message to adapt only after verifying the observation and lawful contact route:

Hi [name], I noticed that on mobile your website asks visitors to open an email app to request a quote. I help local trades businesses collect the job details on the site and send them to the owner by text and email. Would a short example be useful? If this is not relevant, let me know and I will not follow up. Oscar, Tandem [business contact details and privacy link].

This is draft wording, not permission to send a campaign or a guarantee of legal suitability in every case. Do not claim to have found a defect unless it exists.

Proposal and close

When a relevant owner is interested, show the working demonstration and ask whether its workflow matches their need. Verify access and platform constraints. Send a written scope using the template. Explain setup and running costs together.

Offer a paid pilot with a clear end/review point. A reduced pilot price must not require a positive review. Obtain separate permission before using client names, screenshots, metrics or testimonials.

Record the sales funnel

Track relevant businesses considered, permitted contacts made, conversations, qualified needs, proposals, paid pilots and reasons for rejection. Record time spent as well as fees collected. Keep this real lead data outside the public repository.

After a small batch, inspect why people decline: no problem, bad timing, insufficient trust, existing functionality, unclear scope or affordability. Do not assume every rejection means lower prices are needed. Do not scale volume while the offer, delivery or compliance process is unresolved.

SEO GEO and the Grokbot brief

Objective

Attract business owners who need help with enquiry handling. This is different from helping a plumbing client rank for homeowner searches such as “plumber Chichester”. Measure qualified enquiries and customers, not just impressions or mentions in AI responses.

Search visibility is a longer-term acquisition experiment. Initial customer conversations and paid pilots should proceed while useful content and references develop.

Foundations

The original site already contains readable HTML, titles, canonicals, structured data, robots directives, a sitemap and llms.txt. Those assets do not establish actual indexing, traffic or rankings. The Tandem concept intentionally has noindex,nofollow and is not the production SEO configuration.

Before a real launch: choose and clear the domain, configure HTTPS and redirects, verify Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit the intended sitemap, inspect important URLs, and ensure structured data matches the visible approved offer. Exclude archive, documentation, test environments and fictional demos from production indexing.

Google says its AI search features use the normal SEO foundations and do not require special AI files or additional schema. Eligibility and inclusion are not guaranteed. Keep useful content accessible in text and use structured data accurately rather than treating llms.txt as a ranking mechanism. Google AI features guidance.

Operations privacy and launch readiness

Set up a deliverable service

Before handling a client’s live enquiries, agree scope, acceptance, access, account ownership, fees, support hours and incident responsibilities. Use a suitable written agreement rather than treating the website price list as the entire contract.

The original privacy notice describes Formspree, Zoho, Emelia, Airtable and AI research tools. That description must be checked against what is actually configured. Do not copy the notice into Tandem and assume it covers new providers, workflows or tracking.

Business administration

  • Confirm the trading name and legal identity on contracts, invoices and communications.
  • Complete domain and trademark checks before spending significantly on the Tandem brand.
  • Set up appropriate bookkeeping, invoicing, payment collection and tax arrangements.
  • Resolve a suitable service/contact address and applicable public disclosures.
  • Review employment terms and keep employer systems, time and information separate.
  • Consider appropriate professional indemnity and cyber cover for the actual services supplied.

GOV.UK specifies the required information on invoices, including an address where legal documents can be delivered for a sole trader using a business name. It also provides the steps for registration, record keeping and tax planning. Invoice requirements, sole-trader setup.

Client data

Document who is the controller and who processes data on their behalf for each workflow. Where a controller uses a processor, an appropriate written contract is required; sub-processing arrangements also need appropriate terms. Specify instructions, security, assistance, retention, end-of-contract handling and relevant provider arrangements. A marketing-site privacy notice is not a substitute for this. ICO contracts guidance.

Keep records of providers, purposes, data categories, retention, locations/transfers and access. Use minimum necessary fields. Check applicable data-protection fee/registration obligations. Obtain suitable professional advice where the actual operating arrangement requires it.

Security and reliability

  • Use client-owned accounts where practical, scoped access and multi-factor authentication.
  • Store API credentials in provider secret stores or suitable environment configuration; never in public HTML, JavaScript or Git history.
  • Restrict customer records to authorised users and separate clients’ access.
  • Maintain backup/export and recovery procedures appropriate to the storage service.
  • Log failures without unnecessarily copying personal information into alerts.
  • Test delivery after configuration changes; monitor agreed failure and usage signals.
  • Keep a provider outage and incident escalation procedure, including who informs the client.

AI and messaging boundaries

Use deterministic workflow logic first. If AI is later introduced, define its permitted knowledge, actions, escalation and evaluation. An assistant must not invent prices, commitments, booking availability or successful actions. Keep emergency triage and dispatch outside the first scope.

Separate requested service communications from promotional messaging and assess each sequence under the actual circumstances. Do not copy a competitor’s blanket legal description. All customer-facing wording needs approval.

Review requests must invite genuine feedback without incentives or selective positive-review solicitation. Google review policy.

Offboarding

Agree the export format, account transfer, retained legal records, deletion schedule, subscription/number ownership and what service ends. A customer should understand how their enquiry system behaves after support cancellation. Record completion of the handover and revoke unnecessary access.

  • ☐ Hosting arrangement appropriate for commercial use.
  • ☐ Live enquiry route tested to a controlled inbox and number.

Problems found and fixed

  • Search with no results still continues. Make’s Airtable search passes on one summary bundle (“Total number of bundles: 0”) when nothing matches. A counting step added in between counted that summary as 1 and blocked every new enquiry. Fixed by removing the counting step and filtering on Total number of bundles = 0.
  • Tally sends answers as a list. Answers arrive under data.fields, each with a label and a value. Mapped by question label so the mapping survives reordering; labels in Tally must match exactly.
  • Typed values starting with /. Make treats / as the start of a keyword, so values must be typed without it.
  • Placeholder text in the email. Square-bracket placeholders were sent literally until replaced with mapped fields.

Limitation: “Sent” is not “delivered”

With the recipient changed to an address that cannot receive email, Zoho accepted the message, Make marked the record Sent, and the failure appeared only as a bounce in the sender’s inbox a few seconds later. The record therefore shows that the provider accepted the email, not that the owner received it. This matches the warning in the delivery and test plan about distinguishing provider acceptance from delivery.

Before offering this to a client: confirm the owner’s notification address with a live test at setup, watch for bounces, and add a second route such as SMS. Do not describe Sent as proof of delivery.

  • Make: about 1 credit per step, so roughly 6 to 7 credits per enquiry for the full Stage 1 flow on the free plan (1,000 credits a month).
  • Tally, Airtable and Zoho: no extra cost at test volumes. Airtable started a paid-plan trial automatically; the base is expected to continue on the free plan when the trial ends.
  • Build time: record Oscar’s actual hours in the private log; this feeds decision D04.

Accounts used
